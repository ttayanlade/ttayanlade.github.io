The title of today’s article “The parable of the water Tap” is inspired by an existing article “Parable of the Shower Head” https://www.premiumtimesng.com/opinion/144605-parable-shower-head-pius-adesanmi.html , By the late Dr. Pius Adesanmi. I stumbled upon Dr. Adesanmi circa 2019, a random day in my off-campus room in Akure, Nigeria. I was randomly scrolling through facebook when I saw a devastating news about a plane first as a talk he gave which led me to also read the article. In summary, Dr. Adesanmi talked about the dangers of mediocrity and how Nigerians needed to build a culture of excellence using a tale of his experience with a shower head in a hotel in Abuja. A talk that has been close to my heart ever since. <It may be helpful to watch (read) his talk here as it may provide better context to the following paragraphs. Regardless, I would add a better TLDR for his article at the end of mine>

 

This talk is truly so close to my heart that I can recall my grad school resumption day, I turned on my tap and watched water gush out joy. Yes, I muttered silently, this is the excellence I will uphold, not whatever Alfa Plumber did in our house in Oshogbo. I could do 10 push-ups before Alfa’s tap filled my cup. It was fine then shaa, but now that I have seen better I will never kowtow to the mediocity of artisans anymore!

 

And so I lived my life, day after day, weeks after weeks, years after years, upholding excellence or so I thought. Fast forward to a couple of weeks back, a random afternoon. My somewhat new apartment mate, whom I see once every two days if I’m lucky, mentioned to me. Timi, do you know what is wrong with the tap in the kitchen? I was bewildered, wrong? The tap works. It may sometime look effy but it works. My apt. mate responded, No, it does not work well and I would put in a service request so that it can be checked.

 

His decision was fine with me as the service request is free and I did not have to do anything. 9 days after, I came home in the evening famished, rushing to defrost and warm one of my weekly jollof rice batch. As usual, I routinely wanted to wash my hand, I turned on the tap and …

What?... How was I comfortable with the previous state of the tap? Especially given my history. How long have I endured a bad tap without complaining? When did the transition fro a good working tap to the not so functional one happen? Why didnt I notice? Did I notice and did not find it important to fix? Fixing involved completing a free, simple form, why didnt I just do it? What would have happened if I never got a new apt mate? Was my previous apt mate also fine with the state of the tap? Why was I indifferent to my apt.mate’s conversation about fixing it? Did Nigeria (read nigerians) also lose track of excellence at some point? How do I consciously fight against this sort of mediocity? What do I do now? And so on.

 

 

As of the time that I write this, I have some opinions about some of the questions above. But since the objective of this article was simply to get myself to start writing, I would end it here and would probably make the opinions a different article.

– Dr. Pius Adesanmi’s article was about a time he spent in a high end hotel in Abuja, Nigeria. He complained to the hotel management and they sent someone to take a look. The person turned it on but could not seem to understand Dr Adesanmi’s complaints. Dr. Adesanmi used this to describe a growing concern he observed at the time of a growing mediocrity across different sectors in the country and was urging the audience to reject the culture of mediocity.
